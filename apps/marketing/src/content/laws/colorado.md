## What it is

Colorado's first AI law, SB 24-205, never took effect. It was replaced by **SB 26-189**, signed in May 2026 and in effect from **1 January 2027**. The Attorney General must adopt rules by then [1]. xAI, joined by the US Department of Justice, filed a challenge to the earlier law in April 2026 [2].

According to Venable's summary of the law [1], an employer whose automated decision-making technology "materially influences" a decision about selection for a job must:

- give applicants clear and conspicuous notice before using it;
- after an adverse outcome, explain within 30 days, in plain language, the decision and the technology's role in it;
- let people ask to correct inaccurate personal data;
- offer "meaningful human review" and reconsideration where commercially reasonable, by a reviewer with authority to change the decision;
- keep records of compliance for at least three years.

The Attorney General enforces the law, and there is no private right of action [1].

## What it means if you use software to screen applicants

If a tool ranks, scores or recommends applicants and that output materially influences who you hire, plan for notice, explanations after adverse decisions, a human review route and three years of records.
