# Buyer Pain Points and Sourced Numbers

> Fetched **2026-11-08**. This is the source list for every number the marketing site states. A number is usable only
> when its row says **Verified** and the site words it as the line under its table allows. **Verified but older than
> 2 years** may be quoted only with its year in plain sight, and the notes below advise against it. **Not verified**
> never goes on the site.

Method: each URL was opened with a page fetch and the quoted figure was read on the page. A page that returned an
error, or that did not say what a secondary source says, is marked Not verified, whatever search snippets say.
Competitor facts are from the vendor's own pages, and `competitors.md` (snapshot 2026-10-18) is the earlier read.
Nothing here is about ScreenHire's own results, and the site must not borrow a testimonial or a result from this page.

The status column uses three values: Verified, Verified but older than 2 years, Not verified.

## 1. Real people

Pain: a recruiter at a small company reads hundreds of applications a hire, and some of the people are not who they
say they are.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The average recruiter processes 291 applications per hire | 291 applications per hire | Ashby, "Recruiter Productivity", 2026 Talent Trends Report (author Ben Perry) | 2026 (28 Apr) | https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/2023-recruiter-productivity-trends-report | 2026-11-08 | Verified |
| 31% of US managers who hire say they have interviewed someone using a fake identity | 31% (survey of 3,000) | Checkr, "Hiring Hoax" manager survey | 2025 (16 Sep) | https://checkr.com/resources/articles/hiring-hoax-manager-survey-2025 | 2026-11-08 | Verified |
| 35% say someone other than the listed applicant took part in a virtual interview | 35% (same survey) | Checkr, "Hiring Hoax" manager survey | 2025 (16 Sep) | https://checkr.com/resources/articles/hiring-hoax-manager-survey-2025 | 2026-11-08 | Verified |
| Gartner predicts that by 2028, 1 in 4 candidate profiles worldwide could be fake (as reported by HR Dive) | 1 in 4 by 2028 (a forecast) | HR Dive, "By 2028, 1 in 4 candidate profiles will be fake, Gartner predicts" (Carolyn Crist) | 2025 (8 Aug) | https://www.hrdive.com/news/fake-job-candidates-ai/757126/ | 2026-11-08 | Verified |
| Same Gartner claim, read on Gartner's own press release | 1 in 4 by 2028 | Gartner, "Gartner Survey Shows Just 26% of Job Applicants Trust AI Will Fairly Evaluate Them" | 2025 (31 Jul) | https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them | 2026-11-08 (HTTP 403) | Not verified |

Wording and limits.

- Ashby: "an average of 291 applications per hire (Ashby, 2026)". The data is Ashby's own platform, 109M applications
  and 247K jobs from January 2021 to March 2026, so it describes Ashby customers, not every employer. The report text
  says "compared to roughly 100 in early 2021", but its chart alt text gives 121 for Q1 2021 and a nearby sentence says
  every hire in 2025 took more than 300 applications. Do not state the 2021 comparison. The URL slug says 2023 but the
  page is the 2026 report, so the URL may change. This number is about volume, not fraud, so word it as workload.
- Checkr: "31% of US managers involved in hiring say they have interviewed a candidate who turned out to be using a
  fake identity (Checkr, 2025)". It is a Checkr proprietary survey of 3,000 managers who had been involved in hiring in
  the past 12 months. Field dates are not stated, no research firm is named, and it is self-reported, so say "say", not
  "did". The page calls the group "American managers" in its chart captions. Do not write "31% of candidates".
- Gartner: the claim is stated on the HR Dive page above (body: "1 in 4 candidate profiles worldwide could be fake,
  according to a July 31 report from Gartner"; headline says "will be"). Personnel Today also states it, on 1 Aug 2025
  (https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/by-2028-one-in-four-candidate-profiles-will-be-fake), and links no Gartner page.
  Gartner's own press release (31 Jul 2025, the URL in `competitors.md` source 34) returned HTTP 403 again, so whether
  that release carries the sentence is unconfirmed. HR Dive ties only one survey figure to the report: 6% of 3,000
  candidates said they had taken part in interview fraud. The 1 in 4 is a forecast with no method given. The site may
  say "Gartner predicts, as reported by HR Dive (2025)", never "1 in 4 candidates are fake" and never as Gartner's
  measurement.

## 2. Never guessing

Pain: a candidate applies and hears nothing, and the recruiter cannot say who last wrote to them.

| Claim as the site may state it | Number | Publisher and title | Year | URL | Fetched | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 59% of US workers say they rarely hear back after applying, and 33% say they never do | 59% and 33% | Monster, "The Great Job Search Struggle: Nearly All Workers Are Hunting for New Roles and Getting Ghosted" (2024 poll) | 2024 | https://www.monster.com/career-advice/research/job-search-fatigue-poll-2024 | 2026-11-08 (read in a browser) | Verified |
| 75% of job applicants never heard back from the employer | 75% | CareerBuilder survey of 3,900 US workers, read in HR Reporter, "Three-quarters of job applicants don't hear back from employers: Survey" | 2013 (21 Feb) | https://www.hrreporter.com/news/hr-news/three-quarters-of-job-applicants-dont-hear-back-from-employers-survey/284874 | 2026-11-08 | Not verified |
| Recruiters lose time to status chasing, or do not know who last contacted a candidate | none found | none | none | none | 2026-11-08 | Not verified |

Wording and limits.

- Monster, verified on 2026-11-08 in a browser, where the poll page reads: "The majority of workers, 59%, say they rarely
  hear back after submitting applications, and one-third (33%) say they never do", from "a poll conducted by Monster in
  November 2024 among more than 1,400 U.S." workers. The site states it that way. Recheck it in November 2026, when the
  poll is two years old. The note from before it was verified follows.
- Monster: both Monster pages that carry the figure (the poll page and the 2025 WorkWatch report page) returned HTTP
  403, so I could not read the numbers. Two web searches returned excerpts that quote the page as "59% rarely hear back
  and 33% never", a Monster poll run in November 2024 among "more than 1,400 U.S. workers". That is the search tool
  reading the page, not me, so the row stays Not verified. If the owner opens the page in a browser and confirms it, the
  wording would be "59% of US workers say they rarely hear back after applying and 33% say they never do (Monster poll
  of more than 1,400 workers, November 2024)". By next November that poll is two years old, so it needs a recheck then.
- CareerBuilder: the 75% is real but old and second-hand. HR Reporter (21 Feb 2013) says "Three-quarters (75 per cent)
  of job applicants in the United States said they never heard back from the employer", from a CareerBuilder survey of
  3,900 American workers. HR Reporter gives no field dates, and CareerBuilder's own release was not fetched. A search
  summary put the fieldwork in November 2012, which I did not confirm. It is over 13 years old. Do not use it.
- Status chasing: no sourced figure verified. Searches found a survey of 475 recruiters in Ireland run by a job board
  (3.6 hours per vacancy on follow-up and decisions with hiring managers, 22 hours of manual tasks per vacancy), but
  the page timed out twice and it measures hiring-manager follow-up, not candidate status. Robert Half 2021 research
  on candidates dropping out without an update was not opened. The site makes no claim here beyond the candidate-side
  number above, and only once Monster is confirmed.
- Newer ghosting figures (2025) turned up in search summaries: Greenhouse and The Interview Guys put ghosting after an
  interview at about 61% to 63%, and Fast Company reports "80% of jobseekers apply and hear nothing". I opened what I
  could on 2026-11-08: the Fast Company page returned HTTP 403, and Greenhouse's 2025 AI in Hiring release (19 Nov 2025,
  4,136 respondents) states no ghosting figure at all. So none of them is Verified, and the site states no number for
  this pain until someone reads the Greenhouse report itself.

Wording and limits.

- Cornerstone: it is 11 years old, a focus group of 53 in-house recruiters in Europe (London dateline), run by a
  software vendor. HR Review does not state the region. Do not use it as a headline. If used at all: "a Cornerstone
  OnDemand focus group of 53 European in-house recruiters found almost two hours a day went on admin (2015)".
- JobSync and Talent Board: a global survey of nearly 250 recruiters and talent acquisition and HR professionals,
  released 5 Oct 2022 by a vendor and a non-profit, survey dates not stated. Four years old, so quote it with "2022".
- GoHire, what the pages say on 2026-11-08: the pricing card for Pro lists "Automated Stage Actions" and the Starter and
  Growth cards do not (Starter re-read for the compare page the same day). The comparison table's plan markers did not come through, and the help article does not name a plan, so
  "Pro-only" is not proven, only "Pro includes it". The help article says rules (send email, add note, send
  questionnaire, "etc") run "automatically" when a candidate is moved into a stage. That is a trigger on stage change,
  so `competitors.md`'s "no event triggers" is too strong. Say "stage actions that run when a candidate moves stage".
  A second help article says GoHire cannot filter candidates by screening answers ("does not presently provide the
  functionality to filter candidates based on their responses"), which supports "no knockout filters" (page read
  2026-11-08, undated).
- The competitor snapshot in `competitors.md` is dated 2026-10-18, and the re-read above is 2026-11-08. Naming a
  competitor on the marketing site is a legal and brand choice for the owner. Prefer a category line over a name.

## 4. Integrations

Pain: tools that do not talk to each other, or that have no API to build on.

| Claim as the site may state it | Number | Publisher and title | Year | URL | Fetched | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| For one quarter of surveyed recruiting professionals, the ATS is not well integrated into the rest of their tech stack | one quarter; 16% for screening and assessment tools; 15% each for CRMs, background screening and virtual event tools | JobSync and Talent Board, "Are Recruiting Technologies Delivering on Their Promise and Potential?" | 2022 (5 Oct) | https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-research-from-jobsync-and-talent-board-integration-of-critical-recruiting-technologies-remains-spotty-301640839.html | 2026-11-08 | Verified but older than 2 years |
| GoHire has no public API | none | GoHire, pricing page, features page, help centre | 2026 | https://gohire.io/features | 2026-11-08 | Not verified |

Wording and limits.

- JobSync and Talent Board: "In a 2022 survey of nearly 250 recruiting professionals, one in four said their ATS was
  not well integrated with the rest of their tools (JobSync and Talent Board)". Vendor and non-profit research, global,
  dates not stated.
- GoHire API: this is a claim about something absent, and I found nothing that proves it. The pricing page and the
  features page do not mention an API or webhooks (the only "APIs" text on the features page is a footer credit to
  another company). GoHire does advertise a Zapier integration ("Send candidates directly to GoHire from anywhere with
  Zapier"), Slack notifications and "15+ hiring integrations". Do not say "no API". At most: "its own site does not
  describe a public API (checked 2026-11-08)", and prefer not to name it.
- Other figures seen only in search summaries and not opened: 60% of recruiters cite poor integration as the greatest
  HR technology frustration (Aptitude Research), 46% of 1,000 US HR leaders call their sourcing tools ineffective
  (TestGorilla), 75% cite a lack of integration (Jibe). They are not usable.

## 5. Built your way

Pain: software priced and shaped for big teams, with no room for a small team's own process.

| Claim as the site may state it | Number | Publisher and title | Year | URL | Fetched | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ashby's entry plan is $400 a month, for companies of up to 100 employees | $400 a month | Ashby, pricing page (Foundations plan) | 2026 | https://www.ashbyhq.com/pricing | 2026-11-08 | Verified |
| Workable's Standard plan is $299 a month, for 1 to 20 employees | $299 a month ($3,588 a year) | Workable, pricing page (Standard plan) | 2026 | https://www.workable.com/pricing | 2026-11-08 | Verified |
| SmartRecruiters starts at $14,995 | $14,995 | SmartRecruiters, pricing page | 2026 | https://www.smartrecruiters.com/pricing | 2026-11-08 | Not verified |

Wording and limits.

- Ashby: "from $400 a month (Ashby pricing page, 2026)". The page offers monthly or yearly terms with "10% discount for
  annual commitments available", Foundations is "Up to 100 employees", and the next plan is for 101 to 1,000.
  The page does not say whether $400 is the monthly-term price. Say "from".
- Workable: "from $299 a month (Workable pricing page, 2026)". The page shows "$299/month" beside "$3,588/year" and a
  toggle "Monthly / Annually - SAVE 20%". It does not state the monthly-term price, and $299 is most likely the annual
  rate. Pricing for larger headcounts needs the dropdown and is not stated, so do not generalise beyond 1 to 20
  employees. Workable's AI Agent credits are sold apart (5,000 for $600).
- SmartRecruiters: the pricing page I fetched shows no price at all and sends buyers to "Talk to an expert". Search
  results from third-party sites (Pin, Compono and others) list an Essential plan at $14,995 a year, but I did not open
  them and none is the vendor. Do not use $14,995. If the site needs an enterprise contrast, use Ashby and Workable.
- Pricing pages here are rendered by script and change. Recheck before launch.

Wording and limits.

- Manatal: Professional is "$15/per user per month" and the FAQ says billed annually. The page also shows $19, $39

- Zoho: the pricing page text I could read had no per-recruiter price (only a calculator showing "$200" with no inputs).
  Zoho's help centre lists the Staffing Agency edition at $30, $60 and $90 per recruiter per month, billed monthly.
  Third-party sites say $25 on annual billing, which I did not confirm on a Zoho page. Do not use $25 and do not

  Growth 10 and Pro unlimited. The help article is undated, so "2026" is the fetch year.
- GoHire closed jobs: the FAQ reads "An active job is any job you are actively recruiting for, either active or
  closed", and you archive a job to stop charges while keeping its data. Say "a closed job counts toward the limit
  until it is archived".
- Gem: the help article says "Your team must have purchased fraud detection checks" and gives no price. The pricing
  page lists the "AI Fraud Detection Agent" under "Add what you need" with no price. The $1.80 per check in

  month, yearly term 10% off) is ambiguous on the page, so do not quote it.
- Greenhouse: the Plus and Pro statement is on the identity verification article only ("Available for Plus and Pro
  subscription tiers"). The tiers for the rest of Real Talent (matching, fraud detection, spam blocklist) are not
  stated on the pages I read, and the launch post (3 Jun 2025) names no tier, so `competitors.md`'s "Real Talent only on
  Plus/Pro" is narrowed here to identity verification.

  as "reported by HR Dive".

  given). Do not use.

  Robert Half candidate-drop-off figures (found in search summaries, never opened).
