Design board, November 2026

Hiring setup a plumbing firm can finish on its own

Seven surfaces, two or three directions each. Every direction is drawn in the Warm Grid tokens, in light and dark, with the rule it bends named on its strip. Pick one per surface with its Pick button; your picks gather at the end, ready to copy.

Who we design for

Dana Whitfield runs Copperline Plumbing and Heating in Leeds: nine staff, two vans, no HR team. She is hiring a service plumber between jobs, on her phone and a laptop in the office.

Who still gets the power

Everything Dana sets in plain words is the same workflow the API, the MCP server and the graph read. Power users keep every trigger, condition and webhook; they just stop being the first thing anyone sees.

What never changes

Checks suggest a closer look, never a rejection, and the builder never offers one. Levels are words, never scores. A person decides every outcome.

1

Hiring template overview

"When it comes to templates the desire was to have an easy to view/consume look at a job template. Right now the vertical view with all the options the user has to fill out would be a confusing endeavour for a non-technical person."Owner, on today's Pipeline tab: a list of Name, Kind and Time limit rows, then a Workflow automations list

In every direction

The template opens on a new Overview tab that reads the template back in plain words. The tabs become Overview, Pipeline, Questions, Automations and Test (Questionnaire, Graph and Simulate renamed). The Workflow automations list under the stages goes: automations live on their own tab.

Example data

Copperline Plumbing's "Service plumber", copied from the starting point "Trade role with a trial shift": Applied, Phone call, Trial shift, Offer, then the three end stages.

A

Setup checklist

The overview is five steps in the order a person finishes a template, one open at a time, each saying what is set in one line. Great for a first template; it reads less well once everything is done.

B

Candidate journey

Recommended

The template reads as what happens to an applicant, stage by stage: what they are asked, what the team does, what runs on its own. Anything unfinished is a dashed "add" in the place it belongs, so the template fills in as Dana goes and still reads well when it is done.

Pressing a stage opens the Pipeline sheet for it (surface 2). Links go to the tab that holds the thing.Narrow screens and long pipelines fall back to the vertical list, as Stage flow does today.

C

Guided setup

Bends S7: a step list beside the form

A new template is a short interview, one question per screen, with suggestions for the trade picked at the start. Fastest to a first draft; afterwards the template opens on the tabs, so it adds a second way in to maintain.

2

Pipeline as a visual stage editor

"We may want to have Pipeline just be the visual editor for configuring the stages of the template/pipeline."Owner

In every direction

Stage kind leaves the screen. A stage's name sets its kind behind the scenes (Phone call is screening, Trial shift is an assessment), and "More options" holds it for anyone who wants it, with the API unchanged. The end stages are fixed at the end and never need setting up.

Time limit in plain words

"Time limit (days)" becomes a choice of 2 days, 3 days, 5 days or 1 week, with one line under it: the team gets a reminder, the candidate never sees it.

A

Stage cards with a side sheet

Recommended

The stages look like the job's board columns Dana already knows. Press a card and a sheet opens with three plain fields; the row stays in view behind it. It is the pattern the graph and the scorecards already use, so there is one editor to build.

Reorder with the grip: drag it, or press it for Move left, Move right, Move to start, Move to end (A7).A plus on the line inserts a stage there. On a phone the cards stack one per line.

B

A line of stops, edited in place

The pipeline is a route with stops, and the end stages branch off where anyone can leave. The chosen stop opens a strip of fields right under the line, so nothing covers the picture. Lightest to look at; the strip gets cramped on a phone and when a stage needs more fields.

C

Ready-made steps on a shelf

Adding a stage means choosing a familiar step, and the step brings its own time limit and scorecard. Dana never types a kind or a number to get started. The shelf takes room above the pipeline on every visit, even when nobody is adding anything.

Press a tile to add it at the end, or drag it between two stages. Each tile is a button, so dragging is never the only way (WCAG 2.5.7).

3

Automations, renamed from Graph

"This is a much better presentation layer visually and allows for more complex visualizations and customizations. But when a user wants to configure a change, there's a lot of inputs/data for the user to parse through."Owner, on today's form: Trigger, Match, Fact, Test and Value rows with a Not checkbox, then Action N and its field

One way to build, in every direction

An automation is one sentence with blanks: When [something happens] and [this is true], [do this]. Each blank is a button that opens a short list of plain choices. Today's full form stays one press away under "Edit as rules", for power users, and the API is unchanged.

The guardrail, built into the choices

Once a blank reads one of ScreenHire's checks, the "do this" list holds only Ask a teammate to review, Add a tag, and Move to a stage whose own automations are all allowed after a check. Turning someone down, emails, interviews and Offer are not in the list, and one line says why. AI use and accommodation answers are never offered as conditions.

A

Sentences, edited in the list

Bends A6: buttons inside a sentence

The tab is a list of sentences grouped by when they run; opening one turns its words into blanks to change in place. Reads like a set of house rules. The map is a toggle away for anyone who wants the picture.

The blanks keep a border and a chevron, so they read as buttons and never as badges (principle 4).

B

Recipes first

The tab opens on ready-made automations, grouped by what a small team wants done, written for this template's own questions and stages. Most people never write one; "Write your own" opens the sentence. Easiest start, weakest overview of what runs where.

C

The map, edited as sentences

Recommended

Keeps the graph the owner likes as the overview, with each node in short plain words. Pressing a node opens a sheet with that automation as one sentence instead of the long form, and Add automation opens B's recipes. Overview and simple editing in one place, built on today's graph.

A phone shows the List view, since the map is for reading there (section 8).The sheet here edits a check-driven automation, so its "then also" list shows the guardrail.

4

Questions, renamed from Questionnaire

"Lots of whitespace in the questionnaire tab. Very confusing UI/UX: what does Apply form do vs New questionnaire (default form name?) and Add follow-up questionnaire?"Owner. On AI use while applying: "I'm not sure this needs to be configurable; it should just be supported/default by ScreenHire as a mainstay feature."

A form later is named by when it goes

"Add follow-up questionnaire" becomes "Ask more later", which first asks when: when they reach Trial shift, when they reach Offer, or some days after a stage. ScreenHire then makes the form, names it after that moment ("Before your trial shift") and adds the automation that emails it. No more "New questionnaire", and no form that never sends.

AI use is always asked, set once

The AI-use question leaves the template. Every application shows the organization's policy before it starts and asks it at the end. The policy (Allowed, Allowed with disclosure, Not allowed) lives in Verification, Policy, and no automation reads the answer.

A

Forms on the candidate's timeline

Recommended

A strip across the top shows every form at the moment it reaches the candidate, so "apply form" and "emailed later" explain themselves. Under it, each question is one wide row: its words on the left and what the candidate sees on the right, which fills the width without a second pane. Preview stays one press away (A8).

B

Edit the real form

Breaks A8: the edit view is the preview

Dana edits the page the candidate sees, at full width in Copperline's colours: press a question and type over it, with its few settings in a bar above. Nothing to imagine, nothing to switch. Settings beyond the bar (knockout, ranges, file types) need a sheet, and the edit outlines sit on the employer's brand.

C

Every question in one table

Bends L1: a picker inside a cell

One list of every question with a "When we ask" column. Moving a question from applying to the trial shift is one choice in its row, and the forms follow from the column. Feels like the spreadsheet an office manager already keeps; it says least about what the candidate actually sees.

5

Problems and testing

"As a trial user, I'm lost. We need to make this simple."Owner, after a blank "Add a tag" step gave "1 problem to fix", a badge that did nothing when pressed, a message about "action 2", and a test that walked through its steps before saying it could not run

Stop the cause, in every direction

A new step that needs one value asks for it as it is added: "Add a tag" opens straight onto the tag's name. Closing it empty leaves the step out, with "Removed the empty step. Undo" in its place, so a blank step never becomes a problem. And a draft with a problem still saves: only Test and Publish wait for the fix (today a draft that does not validate cannot be saved).

Problems in Dana's words

Automation "Queue for review if no right to work", action 2: Leave this out rather than sending it blank.

The tag in "Queue for review if no right to work" has no name. Type one, or remove that step.

The badge stays a badge

Today's "1 problem to fix" is a badge that acts as a button, which principle 4 and A5 rule out. Every direction keeps the status badge, Needs fixes, as plain status beside the title, and adds a real outline button, "Fix 1 thing", that goes to the problem.

A

The test says first what blocks it

The smallest change to today. The Test tab opens on the problem, with the fix right there, and shows the guided test's first step greyed under it so Dana knows what comes next. Every problem also shows on its own blank, in red, wherever that blank appears.

Once fixed, the guided test runs as today: what happened, who the candidate is, what would happen.

B

Ready to publish check

Publish opens one sheet that checks the whole template: each part ticked or flagged, the fix inline, and the sample test as the last line. It gives a trial user one finish line. Problems wait until Publish to be gathered, so a mistake made early is found late.

Publish stays disabled with the reason beside it, which A3 allows because this sheet can enable it.

C

A live test drive

Recommended

No wizard. The Test tab always shows what happens to four sample candidates built from this template, and reruns them after each change, the way validation already runs after a pause. When something blocks it, the tab opens on the fix and the samples wait. "Try your own candidate" keeps today's guided test for power users.

Uses the simulate endpoint that exists today, once per change; evaluation is deterministic and cheap (workflow-engine.md section 4).A sample's "Show in the map" lights its path on Automations.

6

Careers job cards

"Should we include a short description, 3 rows then truncated, in these cards? Just the title + chips seems a little thin."Owner, on the public jobs list, where each card shows the title and its fact chips

Where the words come from

A "Short summary" field in the job editor, filled in from the description's first paragraph until someone changes it, 300 characters at most. The card cuts it at 3 lines with CSS line clamp, so the page still ships no script, and the job page shows it in full.

What stays

The whole card is the one link, named by its title. Chips stay facts, never links (A5). Same candidate tokens, panel surface and shadow; the employer's colours never restyle the card.

A

Summary under the title

Recommended

Title, three lines on what the job is like, then the facts. It reads in the order a candidate decides, and it is the same card at every width, so phone and desktop need no second layout.

B

Facts left, summary right

On a wide screen the title and chips hold the left of the card and the summary the right, so a long list stays short and the facts line up down the page for scanning. On a phone it stacks to A's order with a rule between. Two layouts to keep right, for a gain only wide screens see.

7

Navigation and settings map

"We need to make it clear to the user widely how to access different menus and submenus. Right now, across the board, we don't have that clarity; things are too nested or hidden away."Owner. Today: Jobs, Candidates, Templates, My tasks, Reports, then Views, then Organization, Hiring setup and Integrations at the bottom, each opening its own settings menu

Agreed in every direction

Email domain moves under Organization. Hiring setup becomes Careers site, holding the careers page and branding. Verification leaves Settings for an area of its own, with tabs Overview, Activity log, Usage, Checks, Policy, AI model and Alerts. The AI-use policy from surface 4 lives in its Policy tab.

A bell for setup and alerts

A bell in the top bar holds the setup checklist until it is done, then the alerts: checks that suggest a review, a job board that stopped answering, a webhook turned off. Each line links to the page that fixes it, so a hidden setting is found from what needs doing.

A

Labelled groups, search finds the rest

Closest to today. The sidebar gains group labels that say what each group is for, the settings menu gives every page one line on what is in it, and Ctrl K finds any setting by an everyday word. Clearer, but a person still has to guess which bottom item holds a setting before the menu helps.

B

One Settings home with every setting listed

Recommended

The sidebar holds the work, Verification included, and one Settings item at the bottom. Settings opens a home page that lists every setting with one line on what it does, with a search box that finds them by everyday words. There is one door to remember, and nothing is more than one click from it.

Inside a settings page the breadcrumb reads Settings, then the group, then the page, and the 200px Settings menu lists the group's pages (the S7 exception).A setting someone's role cannot use is left out of the home, never shown disabled (A3).

C

Every page in the sidebar

Bends S9: the sidebar grows with every page

Each sidebar item opens in place to show its pages, so the whole app is one tree and a filter at the top narrows it. Nothing is hidden, and pages lose their own menus and tabs. The sidebar becomes long and scrolls, and it competes with Views for the same space.
